THE BASICS (read this first)
----------------------------
A weather radar is like a torch that sends out radio waves instead of light.
The antenna in the nose sends a short pulse, the pulse hits rain drops, and a
tiny part of it comes back. The radar measures:

  - HOW LONG it took to come back  -> how FAR the rain is.
  - WHICH WAY the antenna pointed  -> the DIRECTION of the rain.
  - HOW STRONG the echo is         -> HOW HEAVY the rain is.

The strength is measured in "dBZ". Bigger number = heavier rain:

  20-30 dBZ  green    light rain
  30-40 dBZ  yellow   moderate rain
  40-50 dBZ  red      heavy rain        (avoid)
  50+   dBZ  magenta  very heavy rain   (avoid, storm core)

Important: the radar sees WATER DROPS, not clouds and not wind. A cloud
without rain is invisible. Clear air turbulence is invisible. Dry hail and
dry snow give only a weak echo.

The beam is like a cone, 3.5 degrees wide. Far away it becomes very tall
(at 40 NM it is about 15,000 ft tall). The radar only sees the weather at the
heights where the beam passes. That is why TILT (aiming up/down) matters so
much.


TURBULENCE (WX+T mode) - short version
--------------------------------------
Real radar: Doppler. It measures how fast the drops move towards or away
from it. If drops close to each other move at very different speeds
(spread of 5 m/s or more), the air is churning: turbulence. Shown magenta,
only up to 40 NM, and only where there is rain (no rain = no drops to
measure). The gain knob does not change it.

Our app: same rules. Magenta within 40 NM, only where there is an echo,
gain does not change it. The turbulence value comes from FlightGear's
weather volumes (turbulence 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.0 in thunderstorms).

Correct?  Yes, close to real.
Could improve: real Doppler turbulence is patchy and moves from scan to
scan; in Realistic mode the app already makes it flicker a little.


GAIN - short version
--------------------
Real radar: CAL = calibrated (colours mean the dBZ numbers above). The pilot
can turn gain up to see weak echoes (snow, light rain far away) or down to
find the strongest core inside a big red area. After use, back to CAL,
otherwise the colours lie.

Our app: CAL, or -10 to +10 dB. Same idea. Correct.


THE THREE CONNECTIONS (reminder)
--------------------------------
  UDP 49100   other simulator / fake plane  ->  app   (where the plane is)
  UDP 5510    app  ->  FlightGear                     (moves FG's aircraft)
  telnet 5401 app  <-> FlightGear                     (reads FG's weather)
