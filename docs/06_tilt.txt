TILT, THE BEAM, AND GROUND ECHO
===============================

PART 1 - WHAT IS TILT
---------------------
TILT = aiming the radar UP or DOWN, like nodding your head.
  Tilt  0      = straight ahead (level)
  Tilt -2      = 2 degrees down
  Tilt +3      = 3 degrees up
  Knob: -15 to +15 degrees.


PART 2 - THE BEAM IS A CONE, NOT A LINE
---------------------------------------
The beam is 3.5 degrees wide. It is thin near the plane and gets very
TALL far away, like torch light on a wall.

  Seen from the SIDE:

            ____------------ top of beam
   PLANE <<
            ----____________ bottom of beam

   Distance     How tall the beam is
   10 NM         3,700 ft
   40 NM        15,000 ft
   80 NM        30,000 ft

Also the EARTH IS ROUND: far away, the ground curves down under the beam
(about 4,200 ft lower at 80 NM). So a level beam is high above the ground
far away.

The radar only sees rain INSIDE the cone. Rain above or below the cone is
invisible.

HOW TILT AND THE CONE GO TOGETHER
  Tilt = where the CENTRE LINE of the cone points.
  The cone is 3.5 deg wide = 1.75 deg ABOVE the centre + 1.75 deg BELOW.

    Tilt -1:   top edge    -1 + 1.75 = +0.75 deg (a little UP)
               centre      -1        = -1    deg
               bottom edge -1 - 1.75 = -2.75 deg (DOWN)

  Easy rule: 1 degree = 106 ft for every NM of distance.
    at 20 NM, 1 deg =  2,100 ft
    at 40 NM, 1 deg =  4,200 ft
    at 80 NM, 1 deg =  8,500 ft
  (plus the round earth: the ground is lower far away, so the beam is
   that much higher above it: +260 ft at 20 NM, +1,060 at 40, +4,230 at 80)

  Plane at 30,000 ft, tilt -1:
    Distance   Bottom edge   Centre      Top edge
    20 NM      24,400 ft     28,100 ft   31,900 ft
    40 NM      19,400 ft     26,800 ft   34,200 ft
    80 NM      10,900 ft     25,700 ft   40,600 ft

  Note: outside the 3.5 deg cone the beam does not stop at once; it gets
  weaker. The app counts these weak edges too (up to 2.6 deg each side).
  AUTO puts this WEAK outer edge on the ground, not the 3.5 deg edge.


PART 3 - EXAMPLE: SAME STORMS, THREE TILTS
------------------------------------------
Plane at 30,000 ft. A storm 80 NM ahead, rain from the ground up to
25,000 ft.

  Tilt +2 (too high):
     beam at 80 NM covers 36,000 ft to 66,000 ft
     storm top is 25,000 ft -> beam passes OVER it -> SCREEN EMPTY.
     DANGER: you think there is no storm.

  Tilt -1 (good, this is what AUTO picks):
     beam at 80 NM covers 11,000 ft to 41,000 ft
     storm 0-25,000 ft -> beam is in the storm -> STORM SHOWN.

  Tilt -3 (too low):
     beam at 80 NM covers -6,000 ft (below ground!) to 24,000 ft
     beam hits the ground -> GROUND ECHO everywhere, the storm is mixed
     with ground and hard to see.

  BUT, a trap: with tilt -1, look at a storm only 20 NM ahead:
     beam at 20 NM covers 24,000 ft to 32,000 ft
     a smaller storm with top at 20,000 ft is UNDER the beam -> MISSED.
     That is why pilots tilt down from time to time to check close
     storms.

  Picture (side view, tilt -1):

   30,000 ft  PLANE >======___________________________  top of beam
                       \___________                 \______
                           \_______  ___ beam ___          \______
   24,000 ft ......... beam bottom at 20 NM               storm 80 NM
                                    \_______                ###
   20,000 ft    ###  <- small storm      \_______           ###
                ###     at 20 NM: MISSED       \______      ###  SEEN
                ###                                  \_____ ###
   ground  ____________________________________________________


PART 4 - HOW REAL PILOTS USE TILT (Airbus advice)
-------------------------------------------------
  - Cruise: aim so a little GROUND echo appears at the far edge of the
    screen. Then you know the beam is low enough to catch storms.
  - Tilt up and down from time to time:
      echo disappears when you tilt up a little -> LOW top (less danger)
      echo stays when you tilt up               -> TALL storm (danger)
  - A quiet screen at high altitude is NOT proof of no storms. The beam
    may be passing over them.


PART 5 - GROUND ECHO (CLUTTER)
------------------------------
When the beam hits the ground, the ground also sends an echo back.
  Mountains, cities   -> strong (yellow/red)
  Flat land           -> weaker (green)
  Water (sea, lakes)  -> almost NOTHING (beam bounces away like light on a
                         mirror) -> so you can see COASTLINES.
  Behind a mountain   -> nothing (the mountain blocks the beam: shadow)

MAP mode uses this on purpose to see the ground (cyan/yellow/magenta).


PART 6 - HOW OUR APP DOES IT
----------------------------
  - Tilt knob -15 to +15 (AUTO off), or AUTO (see 05 file).
  - Beam 3.5 deg wide, strongest in the middle, weaker at the edges
    (the app checks 7 heights across the cone and mixes them).
  - Round earth included.
  - FlightGear weather: each volume has a BASE and TOP height, so the
    beam really misses a storm whose top is below the beam.
  - Simulated storms: stronger storm = taller storm.
  - Ground: with FlightGear real terrain -> mountains bright, water dark,
    mountain shadows. Without it -> a made-up patchy ground.


PART 7 - IS OUR APP CORRECT?
----------------------------
  Tilt -15..+15, beam 3.5 deg            YES
  Beam strongest in the middle           YES
  Round earth                            YES
  Storm missed when beam goes over       YES
  Ground echo, water dark, shadows       YES
  Beam stays level in turns/climbs       NO (app thinks plane is level)
  Small ring of ground echo at the
  distance = your height ("side lobe")   NO
