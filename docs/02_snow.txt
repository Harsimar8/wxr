SNOW
====

1. HOW A REAL RADAR DOES IT
---------------------------
A real aircraft radar CANNOT TELL SNOW FROM RAIN.
It only measures how strong the echo is, so snow is shown in the SAME
colours as rain: green, yellow, red.

Why snow looks different in strength:
  - DRY SNOW (cold, high up): ice reflects about 5 times less than water
    (-7 dB). So dry snow is WEAK: mostly green, or not seen at all.
    Heavy dry snow is at most about 30-35 dBZ (yellow).
  - WET SNOW (melting, in the 1,000 ft or so just below the freezing
    level): snowflakes with a water skin are big and shiny to the radar.
    The echo gets STRONGER (+5 dB or more). This is called the
    "BRIGHT BAND". It can look like a line of yellow/red that is not a
    storm at all.
  - RAIN below that: normal.

Only big ground radars with "dual polarisation" can tell snow from rain.
Airliner radars cannot.


2. HOW THE PILOT DECIDES IT IS SNOW
-----------------------------------
The pilot uses their head, not the radar:
  - Outside air temperature in the cockpit, and the freezing level from
    the forecast.
  - METAR / ATIS / forecast ("-SN" = light snow at the airport).
  - Rule: echo ABOVE the freezing level = ice/snow (weaker).
          echo JUST BELOW it = melting snow (stronger, bright band).
          echo lower down = rain.
  - In snow, the pilot may turn gain UP to see the weak echoes, and tilt
    so the beam is in the snow.


3. DOES FLIGHTGEAR GIVE SNOW?
-----------------------------
YES, BUT ONLY SOMETIMES.
  - Every FlightGear weather volume has "snow" and "snow-flag" values.
    wxradar_export.nas already sends them to the app.
  - FlightGear only fills them when Detailed Weather is built from a
    METAR that contains snow (SN). Then it puts a big box volume under
    the cloud with snow = strength.
  - The ready-made weather types (Thunderstorm, Cold front, ...) never
    make snow, only rain.
  - IMPORTANT: FlightGear decides "snow" ONLY from the METAR text, NOT
    from temperature. If the METAR says SN, the volume says snow even
    where it is +10 C.


4. HOW OUR APP DOES IT
----------------------
Code: radar.py, scan(), option snow_colors. In the window: the checkbox
"Show snow in cyan/white (training only)" under the knobs, OFF by default
(real radars cannot show snow in another colour). Ticked = the "From
freezing level" colours below. (Changed 24 Nov 2026: the old 3-choice box
was replaced by this checkbox; "From weather data" is no longer in the app.)

Strength: FlightGear snow value -> strength 0.15 + 0.65 x snow
  snow 0.5 -> about 30 dBZ (green/yellow)
  snow 1.0 -> about 42 dBZ (red strength)   <- too strong, see 6.
Snow is NOT weakened again above the freezing level (it is already ice).

Colours:
  Checkbox OFF  [default] - "Real radar (by strength)"
      Rain and snow both green/yellow/red by strength. Like a real radar.
  ("From weather data (cyan/white)" - removed from the app, still in
      radar.py: cyan where FlightGear or the METAR says snow. A real radar
      cannot know this.)
  Checkbox ON  - "From freezing level (cyan/white)"
      The pilot's guess. Green/yellow echo where the beam centre is above
      the freezing level -> cyan (snow/ice). In the 1,000 ft below it ->
      white (melting snow). Red and magenta never change (a strong echo up
      there means hail or a storm core - danger must stay visible).
      If the freezing level is unknown, same as "Real radar".

Example: METAR "-SN", freezing level 5,000 ft, you fly at 8,000 ft.
  Real radar:          green patch.
  From weather data:   cyan (FlightGear says snow).
  From freezing level: cyan where the beam is above 5,000 ft.
  If the freezing level were 12,000 ft (warm), "from freezing level" shows
  normal green/yellow, because at 8,000 ft it would be rain.


5. IS IT CORRECT?
-----------------
  Snow in rain colours by strength (default)   Yes, like a real radar
  Dry snow weaker than rain                    Yes
  Snow not weakened again above freezing       Yes
  Bright band (+5 dB below freezing) for rain  Yes
  Bright band for snow volumes                 No (see S3)
  Heavy snow strength                          Too strong (42 dBZ)
  Cyan/white colours                           Only as a learning option


6. WHAT COULD BE IMPROVED
-------------------------
  S2. Make heavy snow weaker: snow 1.0 should give about 35 dBZ (yellow)
      at most, not 42 (red).
  S3. Wet snow: in the 1,000 ft just below the freezing level, make snow
      echo stronger (bright band), like the app already does for rain.
  S4. Check FlightGear against temperature: if FlightGear says snow but
      the beam is far below the freezing level (warm), treat it as rain.
      This fixes FlightGear's "METAR only" snow.


7. WHAT MORE FLIGHTGEAR CAN GIVE
--------------------------------
  - Temperature at each altitude (environment config "aloft" table and
    /environment/temperature-degc): the app uses this for the freezing
    level. Could also show the outside air temperature on screen.
  - /environment/metar/snow-norm: how heavy the snow in the METAR is.
  - Dew point / humidity: wet bulb temperature tells better where snow
    melts (snow can survive a little above 0 C in dry air).
  - A Nasal script could set the snow value of volumes from temperature
    instead of from the METAR text.
