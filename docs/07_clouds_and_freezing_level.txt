0. THE WHOLE IDEA IN PICTURES
-----------------------------

(a) WHAT FLIGHTGEAR MAKES (Detailed Weather, in every tile)
    -------------------------------------------------------

  FlightGear builds weather
     |
     +-- CLOUDS      what you SEE        -> used for: WHERE, SHAPE, TOP,
     |                                                STORM or not
     +-- VOLUMES     what the plane FEELS -> used for: HOW STRONG
     |   (invisible boxes: rain, snow,               (rain, snow, turbulence)
     |    turbulence, visibility)
     +-- STATIONS    temperature, dew     -> used for: FREEZING LEVEL
     |                point, pressure                 PER AREA
     +-- wind        per tile             -> (not used yet)
     +-- thunderstorm list (only some     -> (not used: missing in tropical
     |   weathers)                            and cold front)
     +-- lightning                        -> last strike only (as before)

  Basic Weather -> none of this -> app guesses from the METAR (old way)


(b) VOLUMES vs CLOUDS
    ------------------

  Volume says:  "it rains 0.4 somewhere in this 18 km circle"
     |
     +-- shape  : perfect circle / square      (not real)
     +-- place  : rough (a big area)
     +-- top    : often wrong (1,500 ft or always 20,000 ft)
     +-- storm? : does not say

  Clouds say:   "these cloud pieces are exactly here, this big, this high,
                 this type"
     |
     +-- shape  : real outline
     +-- place  : exact (matches the window)
     +-- top    : real height
     +-- storm? : YES if a Cumulonimbus is among them
     +-- rain?  : does NOT say -> strength still from the volume

  So:  CLOUDS + VOLUMES together = best radar picture


(c) WHICH WEATHER BELONGS TO STORMS
    --------------------------------

  Weather
     |
     +-- WINDSHEAR (microburst)  -> comes from storms (Cb)  -> needs CLOUDS
     +-- HAIL                    -> comes from storms (Cb)  -> needs CLOUDS
     +-- strong TURBULENCE       -> mostly storms            -> needs CLOUDS
     |
     +-- SNOW                    -> ANY rain cloud when COLD
                                    NOT only storms          -> needs
                                                               TEMPERATURE
                                                               (stations)


(d) SNOW vs HAIL - same line, different question
    ---------------------------------------------

  Freezing level (0 degC line)
     |
     +-- SNOW asks: "is the BEAM above or below the line, HERE?"
     |      |
     |      +-- above the line            -> ice/snow: weaker echo, cyan
     |      +-- 1000 ft below the line    -> melting snow: brighter, white
     |      +-- further below             -> rain: normal
     |
     +-- HAIL asks: "does the STRONG echo (45 dBZ) reach FAR above the line?"
            |
            +-- 4,600 ft or more above    -> HAIL icon
            +-- less                      -> no icon
            +-- not a storm at all        -> no icon (needs a Cb)


(e) HOW A PILOT TELLS SNOW FROM HAIL
    ---------------------------------

  Echo on the radar
     |
     +-- weak, wide, smooth green, cold air      -> probably SNOW
     |      (check: temperature, METAR "SN", look outside)
     |
     +-- strong red/magenta, small, tall
            |
            +-- tilt UP: still red high up        -> HAIL likely
            +-- hook / finger / U / scalloped     -> violent storm, hail
            +-- hail icon (modern radars)         -> HAIL
            +-- rule: stay 20 NM away, never fly under the anvil


(f) FREEZING LEVEL - before and after
    ----------------------------------

  BEFORE
     |
     +-- ONE value near the plane
            +-- 1st: FlightGear temperature table (0 degC crossing)
            +-- 2nd: temperature at the plane - 2 degC per 1000 ft
            |
            +-- used for EVERY storm, near or far   (far storms can be wrong)

  AFTER
     |
     +-- same value near the plane
     +-- each area moved up/down by its weather station:
            near value + (station temp there - station temp here) x 505 ft
              +-- warmer area -> higher freezing level
              +-- colder area -> lower freezing level
     |
     +-- used per area for: ice, bright band, snow colours, hail, top fade


(g) THE PLAN THAT WAS BUILT
    ------------------------

  Change A - CLOUDS
     +-- script sends clouds every 5 s
     +-- app draws echo where clouds are, up to the real top
     +-- Cb cloud = storm -> only storms get hail, windshear, turbulence

  Change B - WEATHER STATIONS
     +-- script sends every tile's station every 5 s
     +-- app gives each area its own freezing level

  Both: switch off in the app -> old behaviour. No data -> old behaviour.
  (Full before/after details: 08_before_and_after_changes.txt)


1. WHAT FLIGHTGEAR MAKES WHEN IT BUILDS WEATHER
-----------------------------------------------
FlightGear's Detailed Weather does not make weather for the whole world.
It makes it in TILES (squares of about 40 x 40 km) around the plane.
When you fly on, new tiles are made AHEAD and old ones BEHIND are deleted.

        +-----+-----+-----+
        |tile |tile |tile |
        +-----+-----+-----+
        |tile |  P  |tile |     P = plane. About 9 tiles around you.
        +-----+-----+-----+
        |tile |tile |tile |
        +-----+-----+-----+

So the radar has real FlightGear weather only up to about 30-40 NM.
At 160 / 320 NM range most of the screen is empty, and storms "pop up"
when a new tile is made.

In EACH tile FlightGear makes (checked in fgdata Nasal\local_weather\):

  Name (in Nasal)          What it is                         Every weather?
  -----------------------  ---------------------------------  --------------
  cloudArray               every cloud you SEE: type,         YES
                           lat, lon, alt, width, height
  effectVolumeArray        invisible boxes: rain, snow,       YES
                           turbulence, visibility, lift
  weatherStationArray      one station per tile: temperature  YES
                           T, dewpoint D, pressure p, vis
  tile wind                wind per tile (depends on the      YES (setting)
                           "wind model" setting)
  windIpointArray          wind at 9 heights at points        only some wind
                                                              settings
  atmosphereIpointArray    cloud layer heights, vis aloft     most
  thunderstormArray        list of storms + strength          ONLY in
                           (strength = chance of lightning)   "Thunderstorms"
                                                              and METAR tiles
                                                              (NOT tropical,
                                                              NOT cold front)

These live inside Nasal code, NOT in the property tree. So only a SCRIPT
(like wxradar_export.nas) can take them out.

3. WHY VOLUMES ARE NOT ENOUGH
-----------------------------
Volumes DO have a position, but they are made for the PLANE'S EFFECTS
("you are inside: start rain and shaking"), not to look like a radar.
Example from volume_current.txt (tile type low_pressure):

   volumes 1,3,6 : 28 x 28 km SQUARE box, rain 0.1 everywhere -> green square
   volumes 4,7   : 18 km CIRCLE, rain 0.4 everywhere          -> yellow disc
   volumes 0,2,5 : 36 x 36 km box, no rain (visibility only)  -> nothing

Problems:
  1. Wrong SHAPE  - real rain is never a perfect square or circle.
  2. FLAT strength - same rain everywhere, no red core in the middle.
  3. ROUGH place  - "it rains somewhere in this big box", not where the
                    actual clouds are. Radar may not match the window.
  4. Wrong HEIGHT - tops like 1500 ft or 13700 ft do not show the real
                    storm top.

   Volume (box):                    Clouds inside it:
   +------------------+             +------------------+
   |                  |             |  oo         o    |
   |   rain 0.1       |             |  ooo             |
   |   everywhere     |             |          oo      |
   |                  |             |    o     ooo     |
   +------------------+             +------------------+
   radar: flat square               real rain: only under the clouds

Clouds tell WHERE + SHAPE + HEIGHT. Volumes tell HOW STRONG.
Best: use BOTH.


4. HOW THE FREEZING LEVEL IS FOUND NOW
--------------------------------------
Code: basic_weather.py freezing_level_ft()

  Way 1 - FlightGear's temperature table (fg_link.py reads it):
            /environment/config/aloft/entry[0..4]/temperature-degc
            /environment/config/boundary/entry[0..1]/temperature-degc
          Find the height where temperature crosses 0 degC:

              12000 ft   -6 degC
               8000 ft   +2 degC   -> 0 degC between them -> about 9000 ft

  Way 2 - no table: temperature at the plane, air is about 2 degC colder
          every 1000 ft:
              plane 5000 ft, +8 degC -> 8 / 2 = 4 -> 4000 ft higher
              -> freezing level about 9000 ft

PROBLEM: ONE freezing level for the WHOLE radar.

   Storm A (40 NM north)        PLANE        Storm B (30 NM south)
   real freezing 7000 ft                     real freezing 11000 ft
   app uses ONE value (near the plane, e.g. 9000 ft) for BOTH.


5. FREEZING LEVEL: THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN SNOW AND HAIL
-------------------------------------------------------
Same line (0 degC), but a DIFFERENT QUESTION.

   height
     |            +---+  storm top
     |            |###|  <- 45 dBZ reaches HERE
     |            |###|     ^
     |            |###|     | 4600 ft or more?  -> HAIL
     | - - - - - -|###|- - -v- - - - - -  FREEZING LEVEL (0 degC)
     |   .........|###|
     |   .........|###|     dots = beam just above/below the line -> SNOW
     +------------------------- ground   colours / bright band

                 SNOW asks                      HAIL asks
  ------------   -----------------------------  ------------------------------
  Question       Is the BEAM above or below     HOW FAR above the line does
                 the line AT THIS SPOT?         the STRONG echo (45 dBZ) reach?
  Looks at       every point the beam hits      one storm, its top part
  Needs storm?   NO - any rain cloud            YES - strong and tall
  Rule           above line: ice, echo -7 dB    45 dBZ at 4600 ft or more
                 weaker, cyan colour            above the line -> hail icon
                 1000 ft below line: melting
                 snow, echo +5 dB brighter
                 ("bright band"), white
  Line wrong by  colour band at the wrong       hail icon MISSING or FALSE
  2000 ft ->     height

In short:
  SNOW = "is it cold HERE?"                      (above or below the line)
  HAIL = "is the storm strong enough HIGH UP?"   (how far above the line)


6. SNOW vs HAIL - WHAT THEY ARE, HOW PILOTS DETECT THEM
-------------------------------------------------------
                SNOW                              HAIL
  ------------  --------------------------------  ------------------------------
  What          soft ice crystals (flakes)        HARD ice balls, pea to golf
                                                  ball or bigger
  How formed    water freezes slowly in cold,     strong UPDRAFT carries drops
                calm clouds                       up and down, ice grows in
                                                  layers like an onion
  Cloud         calm, flat, wide (Nimbostratus)   tall violent thunderstorm (Cb)
  Season        winter, cold air                  also SUMMER (hot ground =
                                                  strong storms)
  Area          BIG: tens to hundreds of km       SMALL: few km under the core
  Danger        low visibility, icing, slippery   breaks windscreen, dents nose,
                runway                            damages engines
  On radar      WEAK echo (green, some yellow)    very STRONG (red, magenta)

How a pilot detects SNOW (radar cannot say "snow"):
  1. Temperature below 0 degC / beam above the freezing level
  2. Weather reports (METAR "SN")
  3. Radar: wide, weak, smooth green area in cold air
  4. Looking outside
  TRAP: snow looks WEAKER than it is - heavy snow can look like light rain.

How a pilot detects HAIL (radar cannot see hail directly):
  1. Very strong echo: red / magenta
  2. TILT UP: storm still red HIGH up (above freezing) = strong updraft
     (our hail icon uses this: 45 dBZ at 4600 ft or more above freezing)
  3. Warning shapes: hook, finger, U-shape, scalloped edges
  4. Storm growing very fast
  5. Hail icon on modern radars (Honeywell IntuVue, Collins MultiScan)
  6. Stay 20 NM away; never fly UNDER THE ANVIL (flat top) - hail can
     fall into clear air where the radar sees nothing.


7. WHICH WEATHER BELONGS TO STORMS (AND WHICH DOES NOT)
-------------------------------------------------------
  Windshear (microburst)   comes from thunderstorms (Cb)    STORMS ONLY
  Hail                     comes from thunderstorms (Cb)    STORMS ONLY
  Turbulence (strong)      mostly storm cores and edges     MOSTLY STORMS
  Snow                     any rain cloud when it is COLD   NOT storms -
                                                            needs COLD AIR

So:
  - windshear, hail, strong turbulence -> need to know WHICH clouds are
    real storms (Cb)           -> CLOUDS help
  - snow -> needs to know the TEMPERATURE in that area
                               -> WEATHER STATIONS help


8. THE CHANGES WE WILL MAKE
---------------------------
CHANGE A - Clouds (cloudArray) for SHAPE, PLACE, TOP, TYPE

  A1. wxradar_export.nas (inside FlightGear)
      - Copy clouds to /wxradar/clouds every few seconds.
      - Only RAIN clouds (storm / rain types), not every small fair cloud
        (there can be thousands).
      - Pack into one text line, like the terrain, so it is fast.
      - FIRST: save them to a text file (like volume_current.txt) to check
        the type names and values.

  A2. fg_link.py
      - Read /wxradar/clouds and unpack into a list.

  A3. weather.py (main change)
      - GROUP nearby rain clouds into one storm.
      - SHAPE  = area covered by the cloud pieces (uneven, real).
      - CORE   = where pieces are most packed -> red / magenta there.
      - STRENGTH = rain / turbulence of the VOLUME at that spot;
                   no volume -> by cloud type (Cb strong, Nimbus medium).
      - TOP    = height of the highest cloud piece.

  A4. main.py
      - Option "Echo shape: from clouds / from volumes".
      - No clouds -> falls back to volumes automatically.

  A5. Things that use storms
      - Hail icon  : only on Cb, using the real cloud tops.
      - Windshear  : microburst only under Cb cores, 1-4 km size.
      - Turbulence : strongest on Cb cores and edges.
      - Beam over the top : uses real cloud tops.

CHANGE B - Weather stations (weatherStationArray) for FREEZING LEVEL PER AREA

  B1. wxradar_export.nas
      - Copy each station: lat, lon, alt, T, D, p -> /wxradar/stations.
  B2. fg_link.py
      - Read the stations.
  B3. basic_weather.py / weather.py
      - Freezing level for each station:
            freezing = station alt + T / 2 degC x 1000 ft
      - For any point: use the nearest station (or blend between them,
        like FlightGear does).
  B4. radar.py
      - Snow colours, bright band, ice weakening and the hail rule use the
        freezing level OF THAT AREA, not one value for the whole radar.
      - Keep the old way (temperature table) as fallback.


9. WHAT EACH CHANGE FIXES (BEFORE -> AFTER)
-------------------------------------------
  Feature       BEFORE (volumes, one freezing)   AFTER (clouds + stations)
  ------------  -------------------------------  ------------------------------
  Storm shape   squares and circles              real uneven outline
  Storm place   somewhere in a big box           where you SEE it outside
  Storm core    flat colour                      red/magenta where clouds packed
  Storm top     volume top (often wrong)         highest real cloud piece
  HAIL          any strong rain box; far storms  only Cb; real top; each storm
                judged by YOUR temperature       judged by ITS OWN freezing lvl
  WINDSHEAR     every rain box with rain > 0.5,  only under Cb cores, 1-4 km,
                9-18 km wide (too big)           like a real microburst
  TURBULENCE    flat over the whole box          patches on storm cores / edges
  SNOW          one freezing level everywhere    freezing level per area; shape
                                                 under real clouds
  Bright band   one height everywhere            right height in each area

  How much it helps:
    Windshear   ***  (biggest)
    Hail        ***
    Turbulence  **
    Snow        *    from clouds,  ***  from weather stations


10. ORDER OF WORK
-----------------
  Step 1  wxradar_export.nas -> save clouds + stations to a TEXT FILE
          (look at the data first; radar does not change)
  Step 2  fg_link.py         -> read clouds + stations
  Step 3  weather.py         -> shape from clouds, strength from volumes
  Step 4  main.py            -> switch clouds / volumes (+ fallback)
  Step 5  hail, windshear, turbulence use storm clouds (Cb)
  Step 6  freezing level per area (stations) for snow, bright band, hail
  Step 7  TEST in FlightGear: compare radar with clouds out of the window,
          try "Thunderstorms", "Cold front", "Low pressure" weather


11. THINGS TO WATCH OUT FOR
---------------------------
  - Cloud TYPE NAMES must be checked in the text file first (which names
    are rain clouds, which are storms).
  - Too many clouds can slow FlightGear: send only nearby rain clouds.
  - Clouds move with the wind: re-send every few seconds.
  - Clouds have NO rain number: echo strength still comes from volumes or
    is decided from cloud type (a model, like the windshear wind).
  - Weather stations only differ if FlightGear's weather differs between
    tiles. With one simple weather everywhere, the result is almost the
    same as now.
  - thunderstormArray is NOT used for shapes (missing in tropical / cold
    front weather). Maybe later only for the lightning icon.


12. WHAT WAS BUILT (Change A + Change B are now in the app)
-----------------------------------------------------------
wxradar_export.nas (copy it again into fgdata\Nasal\ !)
  - Every 5 s: /wxradar/clouds/data   clouds near the plane (up to 4000,
    about 65 NM), without cirrus/altocumulus and other high thin clouds.
  - Every 5 s: /wxradar/stations/data every tile's weather station.
  - Also /wxradar/clouds/count, /wxradar/clouds/total, /wxradar/stations/count
    (check them in the property browser).

fg_link.py
  - Reads both every 5 s (Detailed Weather only): parse_cloud_export(),
    parse_stations().

weather.py (FlightGearWeather)
  - For each rain/snow volume: the clouds inside it (up to 1.3 x its size)
    are spread on a 0.2 NM grid -> "cloud cover" 0..1.
      cover below 0.12          -> no echo
      cover 0.12 .. 1           -> green edge up to the volume's own
                                   strength where clouds are packed
  - Echo top = highest cloud (never lower than the volume's own top).
    The anvil (Stratus (thin)) is not counted: it gives almost no echo.
  - STORM = the volume has a Cumulonimbus / Cb_box / Congestus cloud.
  - Storm CORE = the most packed spot of its clouds.
  - Turbulence (WX+T): only in storms.
  - Microburst (windshear): only under storms, on the core, radius
    0.5 - 1.1 NM (real ones are under 4 km across).
  - A volume WITHOUT clouds keeps its old shape (nothing lost).
  - freezing_at(x, y): freezing level per area =
        freezing level near the plane
        + (station temperature there - station temperature here) x 505 ft/degC
    (FlightGear cools the air 1.98 degC per 1000 ft.)
    So near the plane nothing changes; far areas move up or down.

radar.py
  - Ice (-7 dB), bright band (+5 dB) and "From freezing level" snow colours
    use the freezing level of each point's area.
  - Hail icon: only on storms, on the core, with the real cloud top and the
    storm's own freezing level.

main.py
  - "Shape from FlightGear's clouds"  (Realistic only, on by default)
  - "Freezing level per area"         (on by default)
  - FlightGear panel: "Cloud pieces (export)" = how many clouds came, how
    many volumes they shape, how many storms (Cb) were found.
    "Weather stations" = how many, their temperatures, freezing level range.

Fallbacks (nothing breaks):
  - Old wxradar_export.nas / Basic Weather: no clouds, no stations -> works
    exactly as before.
  - Switch either box off -> old behaviour for that part.
  - "Real data" echo shape -> FlightGear's volumes exactly as given (clouds
    are only used in Realistic).

Tested with made-up FlightGear data (one thunderstorm with Cb clouds, one
big rain box with Nimbus clouds, three stations 16 / 20 / 26 degC):
  - storm found only in the Cb volume; rain box shaped, but no storm
  - hail icon and microburst on the storm core
  - turbulence only in the storm (rain box lost its magenta)
  - freezing level 7,991 - 13,012 ft across the areas (10,000 ft at the plane)
NOT yet tested inside real FlightGear: check the panel numbers there.
