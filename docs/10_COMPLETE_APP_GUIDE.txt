1. WHAT THIS APP IS
==============================================================================
  - An aircraft WEATHER RADAR (Boeing / Airbus ND style) written in Python.
  - It shows storms ahead of the plane in green / yellow / red / magenta.
  - It can run:
       * on its own (built-in weather and a simulated plane),
       * with FlightGear (FlightGear's plane and weather),
       * with ANOTHER simulator (its plane, FlightGear's weather).
  - It copies real radar behaviour: beam width, tilt, gain, attenuation,
    ground clutter, turbulence (Doppler), predictive windshear, hail icon.
  - Start:  pip install -r requirements.txt   then   python main.py

3. HOW A WEATHER RADAR WORKS (BASICS)
==============================================================================
  - The antenna in the nose sends a pulse; rain drops send a little back.
       time to come back   -> how FAR
       antenna direction   -> which WAY
       echo strength (dBZ) -> how HEAVY
  - Colours:
       green    20-30 dBZ   light rain
       yellow   30-40 dBZ   moderate
       red      40-50 dBZ   heavy (avoid)
       magenta  50+   dBZ   very heavy, storm core (avoid)
  - The radar sees WATER DROPS only. Not clouds, not wind, not clear-air
    turbulence. Dry snow and ice give only a weak echo.
  - The beam is a cone 3.5 deg wide: at 40 NM it is about 15,000 ft tall.
    It only sees weather at the heights it passes -> TILT matters.
  - It looks 180 deg ahead (+/- 90 deg). Nothing behind or far beside.
  - Doppler radars also measure how fast the drops move -> turbulence and
    windshear.

4. THE RADAR SCREEN - WHAT YOU SEE
==============================================================================
  - Track-up ND: compass arc, range rings, heading box (TRK ... MAG).
  - White triangle = where the nose points (heading).
  - Magenta notched box = selected heading (heading bug).
  - GS / TAS and wind arrow (top left).
  - Top right: weather source (e.g. "FG VOLUMES 8 SHAPED") and range.
    Amber source text = only approximate.
  - Left: radar mode and tilt (e.g. "WX+T", "-1.5A"; A = AUTO tilt).
  - Echo colours (green / yellow / red / magenta).
  - Magenta in WX+T = turbulence.
  - Red/black striped wedge + amber lines = WINDSHEAR area.
  - Text WINDSHEAR: amber = caution, red = warning.
  - White upside-down triangle with dots = HAIL icon.
  - "GND CLUTTER" = ground echo paints more than 1 % of the screen.
  - STBY / TEST / "WXR TEST" labels in those modes.
  - Sweep: the picture is painted by a moving sweep line, like a real one.
  - Optional cyan/white snow colours (training checkbox, off by default).


==============================================================================
5. THE RADAR CONTROLS ("Weather radar panel")
==============================================================================
  - WXR       EFIS switch: off removes the weather from the ND.
  - Mode      OFF   nothing
              STBY  on, no beam
              TEST  colour test pattern + windshear test icon
              WX    weather
              WX+T  weather + turbulence (magenta) within 40 NM
              MAP   ground mapping (cyan / yellow / magenta)
  - Tilt      -15 .. +15 deg.  AUTO = bottom of the beam on the ground just
              past the selected range (no clutter). Tilt down by hand ->
              ground clutter fills the screen.
  - Gain      CAL (calibrated) or -10 .. +10 dB by hand (VAR).
              Turbulence is NOT changed by gain (like real Doppler).
  - Range     10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320 NM.
  - [x] Simulate attenuation        heavy rain hides what is behind it
  - [x] Windshear voice alerts      spoken PWS alerts
  - [ ] No FlightGear volumes: draw weather from METAR / clouds (fallback)
  - [ ] Show snow in cyan/white (training only)   real radars cannot
  - Save radar image (PNG)

7. WHERE THE WEATHER COMES FROM (ALL SOURCES)
==============================================================================
  The app picks the source by itself (label top right of the screen):

  Label            Source                                     Exact?
  ---------------  -----------------------------------------  ----------
  SIM WX           built-in storms (Simulated mode)           made up
  FG VOLUMES n     FlightGear Detailed Weather volumes        REAL place
     ... SHAPED    same, Realistic shape                      REAL + model
  FG LAYERS        Basic Weather, Manual Configuration         real layers
                   (cloud layers, rain/snow sliders, turb table)
  CLOUD ECHO n     Detailed Weather, no volumes: echo on       approximate
                   each cloud position
  FG METAR         no volumes, no clouds: storms from METAR    approximate
  METAR TEMP       Detailed Weather still building             approximate
  NO VOLUMES /     fallback off, or other-sim mode without     nothing
  DETAILED WX OFF  Detailed Weather
  NO FLIGHTGEAR    other-sim mode, FlightGear not connected   nothing
  WX RESET         FlightGear was reset, reading again        wait

  Built-in scenarios (Simulated mode):
     Clear, Scattered Cb, Squall line, Embedded cell in rain,
     Isolated hook cell, Finger cell, U-shape cell, Scalloped cell
     (the last four = Airbus warning shapes: violent storms).
  Built-in weather controls: Precipitation (rain/snow/mixed), Storm
  strength, Coverage, Wind from / speed, Freezing level, Regenerate storms,
  "Add microburst ahead", "Send weather to FlightGear" (as a METAR).


==============================================================================
8. FLIGHTGEAR: WHAT IT GIVES, HOW WE TAKE IT
==============================================================================
  FlightGear's Detailed Weather makes weather in TILES (about 40 x 40 km)
  around the plane. New tiles ahead, old ones behind deleted. So weather
  exists only about 30-40 NM around you.

  In every tile:
   |
   +-- CLOUDS      what you SEE          -> where, shape, top, storm?
   +-- VOLUMES     what the plane FEELS  -> rain, snow, turbulence strength
   +-- STATIONS    temperature           -> freezing level per area
   +-- wind, thunderstorm list, lightning (partly used / not used)

  How the app takes data:
   +-- GETTING (telnet, fg_link.py): simple properties - position, heading,
   |   speeds, wind, temperature, METAR, temperature table, lightning...
   +-- SCRIPT (wxradar_export.nas, runs INSIDE FlightGear): data hidden in
   |   Nasal code, copied to /wxradar/...
   |      volumes   every 2 s   /wxradar/volumes
   |      clouds    every 5 s   /wxradar/clouds/data   (rain clouds, ~65 NM)
   |      stations  every 5 s   /wxradar/stations/data
   |      terrain   grid 90x90 NM, height + water   /wxradar/terrain/data
   |      placing / removing storms   /wxradar/place
   +-- UDP (generic protocol): the app MOVES FlightGear's plane
       (wxradar-in.xml, other-simulator mode)

  wxradar_export.nas must be in  ...\fgdata_2024_1\Nasal\  (loads at start).


==============================================================================
9. ECHO SHAPE: REAL DATA / REALISTIC / CLOUDS
==============================================================================

  Echo shape
   |
   +-- Real data   FlightGear volumes exactly: one flat colour per volume,
   |               turbulence from its value. Circles and boxes.
   |
   +-- Realistic   same volumes drawn like a real storm:
          |        strong core, weaker ragged edges, weaker near the top,
          |        Doppler-like turbulence patches, F-factor windshear
          |
          +-- [x] Shape from FlightGear's clouds   (default ON)
                 For each rain/snow volume:
                  +-- no clouds inside -> volume's own Realistic shape
                  +-- clouds inside
                        +-- echo WHERE the clouds are, strongest where packed
                        +-- echo top = highest cloud (not the anvil)
                        +-- strength = the volume's value
                        +-- Cumulonimbus / Cb_box / Congestus inside?
                              YES -> STORM: hail check, microburst,
                                     turbulence; core = most packed spot
                              NO  -> normal rain

  Why clouds: volumes are boxes ("rain somewhere here"); clouds show the
  real place and shape, so the radar matches the window.


==============================================================================
10. FREEZING LEVEL
==============================================================================
  Near the plane (as before):
    1st  FlightGear's temperature table (where it crosses 0 degC)
    2nd  temperature at the plane, 2 degC colder per 1000 ft
    Simulated mode: the "Freezing level" box.

  [x] Freezing level per area (default ON, needs weather stations):
    each area = near-plane value
              + (its station temperature - station temperature here) x 505 ft
    warmer area -> higher; colder area -> lower.

  Used for: ice (-7 dB above it), bright band (+5 dB in the 1000 ft below),
  snow colours (training), hail rule, storm top fade.


==============================================================================
11. SNOW
==============================================================================
  - Real radars cannot tell snow from rain: snow is just WEAKER (mostly
    green). The app does the same by default.
  - Ice above the freezing level: echo 7 dB weaker.
  - Melting snow just below it (bright band): echo 5 dB stronger.
  - [ ] "Show snow in cyan/white (training only)" - OFF by default.
        ON: green/yellow above the freezing level -> cyan; in the 1000 ft
        below -> white. Red/magenta stay.
  - Snow is NOT only from storms: any rain cloud when it is cold.


==============================================================================
12. HAIL
==============================================================================
  - No radar sees hail. It is guessed: 45 dBZ reaching 4,600 ft or more
    ABOVE the freezing level = likely hail (Waldvogel 1979, NEXRAD rule).
  - Icon: white upside-down triangle with dots, on the storm.
  - With cloud shapes: only STORMS (Cb), real cloud top, storm's own
    freezing level, icon on the core.
  - Needs the freezing level (no freezing level -> no hail icon).


==============================================================================
13. TURBULENCE (WX+T)
==============================================================================
  - Real radar: Doppler sees drops moving in many directions (spectrum
    width 5 m/s and up). Needs rain. Only within 40 NM. Not changed by gain.
  - App: magenta where turbulence is above 0.5 (= 5 m/s), within 40 NM,
    where there is an echo.
  - VALUE always comes from the FlightGear volume.
  - Realistic: patches, strongest on the core walls, rougher higher up,
    changes from scan to scan.
  - With cloud shapes (current rule): shown only in STORM volumes (Cb).
    A non-storm volume with turbulence shows NO magenta.
    NOTE: this may hide turbulence the plane really feels; a change was
    proposed (show it in all rain, storms just stronger on the walls) -
    not decided yet.


==============================================================================
14. WINDSHEAR (PWS - PREDICTIVE WINDSHEAR)
==============================================================================
  - Real radar: Doppler measures drops coming toward then going away
    (microburst) -> F-factor. F 0.13 or more over about 1 km = danger.
  - App: FlightGear has NO microburst wind, so the app BUILDS one under
    heavy rain (downdraft + outflow), then detects it like a real PWS.
  - Rules (like Boeing):
       above 2,300 ft AGL   off
       1,200 - 2,300 ft     icon only
       below 1,200 ft       icon + text + voice
       looks 5 NM ahead, +/- 40 deg
       AMBER "WINDSHEAR"  "Monitor radar display"   (farther / to the side)
       RED   "WINDSHEAR"  within 3 NM and +/- 25 deg
             descending: "Go around, windshear ahead"
             climbing:   "Windshear ahead, windshear ahead"
             (repeats every 8 s)
  - Which rain makes a microburst:
       Real data:   45 dBZ or more + turbulence 0.6 or more at 500 ft
       Realistic:   rain above 0.5 (stronger with more rain + turbulence)
       + clouds:    only STORMS (Cb), under the core, 0.5-1.1 NM radius
  - Test: Simulated mode, altitude below 1,200 ft, "Add microburst ahead".
  - The plane in FlightGear does NOT really sink (FlightGear has no
    microburst) - only the radar warns.


==============================================================================
15. ATTENUATION, GROUND CLUTTER, TERRAIN
==============================================================================
  - Attenuation: heavy rain absorbs the beam; what is behind looks weaker
    or black ("radar shadow"). Checkbox "Simulate attenuation".
  - Ground clutter: when the beam hits the ground. Stronger the lower the
    beam. "GND CLUTTER" warning. AUTO tilt avoids it.
  - Real terrain (from wxradar_export.nas): 90 x 90 NM grid of height and
    land/water. Hills and mountains bright, water almost nothing
    (coastlines show), mountains hide what is behind them. Unknown areas:
    made-up ground texture.
  - MAP mode: ground mapping, brighter ground, cyan/yellow/magenta.
  - Earth curve is included (the ground drops away from a level beam).


==============================================================================
16. PLACING WEATHER ON THE MAP
==============================================================================
  - "Show weather map" opens a north-up map beside the radar (plane in the
    middle, trail of the last 10 minutes, radar coverage in green,
    mouse wheel = range 5-320 NM).
  - Map click: Off / Add storm / Remove storm.
  - Types:  Rain shower      2 km, rain 40 %,  turb 0 %,  base 3000 ft
            Cumulonimbus     3 km, rain 80 %,  turb 60 %, base 2500 ft
            Thunderstorm     5 km, rain 100 %, turb 80 %, base 2500 ft
    (size, rain, turbulence, base can be changed; rain label shows colour:
     below 32 % green, 32-57 yellow, 57-82 red, above 82 magenta)
  - Simulated: app storm, drifts with the wind.
  - FlightGear: FlightGear builds REAL clouds + volume (needs Detailed
    Weather + wxradar_export.nas). Answer "OK ..." or "FAILED ...".
  - Other sim: only with FlightGear connected.
  - "Hold aircraft still": stops the plane (pauses FlightGear).
  - Remove one / "Clear placed weather" (also in FlightGear).
  - FlightGear deletes a placed storm with its tile when you fly away; the
    map follows.
  - A placed "Rain shower" counts as a STORM with cloud shapes (FlightGear
    builds it from a Cb_box cloud); at 40 % rain it is too weak for hail or
    a microburst.
  - Tested: test_place_map.py ALL OK (echo appears at the right bearing and
    distance; nothing behind; add/remove works).

17. FAKE PLANE AND OTHER SIMULATOR (UDP)
==============================================================================
  - Purpose: use this radar with ANOTHER simulator. That simulator gives the
    plane's position; FlightGear gives the weather.
  - Fake plane = stands in for that simulator while testing.
       Left panel "Fake plane (test)": speed, turn (deg/s), heading,
       altitude, lat/lon, Start / Stop, Move plane.
       Sends 10 times a second: {"lat","lon","hdg","alt","gs","trk"}
       to UDP port 49100. Mode switches to "Other simulator (UDP)".
  - Also as its own program: python fake_plane.py --gs 250 --turn 1
  - With FlightGear connected (--fdm=null): the app moves FlightGear's
    plane to the fake plane 30 times a second (UDP via wxradar-in.xml,
    or telnet as fallback); FlightGear builds weather around it.
  - FlightGear frames/s shown: green 25+, amber 15-25, red below 15.

19. WHAT IS REAL AND WHAT IS A MODEL
==============================================================================
  REAL (from FlightGear data or real radar rules):
    - where the weather is, its strength, its clouds, tops, storm type
    - beam width, tilt, earth curve, ranges, 180 deg coverage
    - attenuation, ground clutter, real terrain and water
    - freezing level (table / stations)
    - hail rule, PWS limits and alerts (2300 / 1200 ft, 5 NM, amber / red)
  MODEL (FlightGear does not have this data):
    - microburst wind (the app builds it)
    - red core inside a storm (FlightGear gives one value per volume)
    - turbulence patches (FlightGear gives one value)
    - rain value 0..1 -> dBZ (FlightGear rain is not dBZ)
    - built-in storms in Simulated mode


==============================================================================
20. LIMITS (WHAT IT CANNOT DO)
==============================================================================
  - FlightGear weather only about 30-40 NM around the plane: long ranges
    (160 / 320 NM) mostly empty; storms "pop up" when a tile is made.
  - No real wind inside storms in FlightGear -> windshear is a model, and
    the plane does not really sink.
  - No pitch / roll yet -> no beam stabilisation in turns.
  - Only the last lightning strike is used, no lightning icon.
  - Basic Weather has no volumes or clouds -> approximate echoes.
  - Cloud shapes and stations: tested with made-up data, not yet in real
    FlightGear.

21. HOW TO RUN IT (STEP BY STEP)
==============================================================================
  A) Alone (no FlightGear)
     1. python main.py
     2. Mode: Simulated. Choose a scenario. Radar mode WX.

  B) With FlightGear
     1. FlightGear launcher > Additional Settings:
           --telnet=5401
           --disable-real-weather-fetch
     2. wxradar_export.nas in fgdata\Nasal (already copied there).
     3. Fly! Then Environment > Weather > Detailed Weather > Thunderstorms
        > Apply. Wait about 30 s.
     4. python main.py  ->  Mode FlightGear  ->  Connect to FlightGear
     5. Echo shape: Realistic. Both boxes ticked.
     6. Panel: check "Cloud pieces (export)" and "Weather stations".

  C) Other simulator / fake plane
     1. FlightGear with the full option list (section 17 / README):
        --fdm=null, --generic=socket,in,30,127.0.0.1,5510,udp,wxradar-in ...
     2. wxradar-in.xml in fgdata\Protocol.
     3. Connect to FlightGear, then Start fake plane (or the other sim).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
27.1  PITCH AND ROLL STABILISATION                          (+2 points)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
  A real radar antenna is held level by the aircraft's attitude (from the
  IRS). When the plane banks or pitches, the antenna moves the other way,
  so the beam stays where the pilot set the tilt - measured from the
  HORIZON, not from the nose.

WITHOUT IT (now) - what is wrong
  The app thinks the plane is ALWAYS level.

    Plane banks 25 deg left in a turn:

    REAL      sky                               beam level everywhere
     \     |     /                          -> picture looks the same
      \    |    /   left wing down:            as in level flight
   ground  |  sky   left side of beam
   (clutter)        hits the ground,
                    right side looks
             plane, no stabilisation      YOUR APP (now)
            over the storms

  - In a turn the app shows a perfect picture, but a real radar WITH
    stabilisation also does (so normal flying looks right).
  - BUT: in a climb (nose 10 deg up) the beam of a real radar is still
    level (stabilised). In the app the tilt is also from level - fine.
  - What is MISSING: the pilot cannot train the "stabilisation failed"
    case, and the app ignores how the plane's attitude really moves the
    antenna limits (a real antenna can only move about +/- 30-40 deg; in
    steep bank or pitch the stabilisation runs out and the picture goes
    wrong - clutter on one side).
  - The app also does not know the real pitch, so AUTO tilt and the
    picture during a steep climb / descent are slightly idealised.

WITH IT - what would be right
  - Read /orientation/pitch-deg and /orientation/roll-deg from FlightGear
    (fast, by UDP or telnet).
  - STAB ON (normal): beam held level, limited by the antenna's real
    movement range -> in very steep bank / pitch one side shows clutter,
    like a real radar.
  - STAB OFF (failure training): beam fixed to the nose -> in a turn the
    low wing side fills with ground clutter, the high side looks over the
    storms. Pilots learn to recognise this failure.

    TREE
    Plane attitude (pitch, roll from FlightGear)
       |
       +-- STAB ON  -> beam = tilt from the horizon
       |                 +-- small bank / pitch -> picture normal
       |                 +-- very steep         -> antenna limit reached,
       |                                           clutter on one side
       +-- STAB OFF -> beam = tilt from the nose
                         +-- bank left  -> clutter on the left
                         +-- pitch up   -> beam over the weather


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
27.2  ATTENUATION ALERT (Collins "PAC", Honeywell "REACT")   (+1.5 points)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
  Heavy rain absorbs the beam. Behind a strong storm the radar is BLIND:
  it shows black or green where there may be another big storm.
  Real radars MARK where they are blind:
    Collins:   yellow arc on the outer edge of the screen ("PAC alert")
    Honeywell: cyan area behind the storm ("REACT")

WITHOUT IT (now) - what is wrong
  The app DOES simulate the loss (checkbox "Simulate attenuation"), but it
  does not tell the pilot.

    Radar now:                       Danger:
                                     behind the red cell there is a
       [   black   ]  <- looks       SECOND storm, but it looks black
         [ RED ]         clear!      -> the pilot thinks it is clear
           ^                         -> flies into the "radar shadow"
          plane

  - Black behind a storm looks like CLEAR SKY. It is not.
  - Airbus training says: never fly into a shadow behind a storm.

WITH IT - what would be right
  - The app already knows how much signal is lost on every beam
    (radar.py, the attenuation part). When the loss is big (e.g. more than
    about 20 dB before the end of the range), mark it.

    Radar with alert:
       ~~~~~~~~~~~~~  <- yellow arc on the edge (Collins style)
       [ blind area ] <- or cyan area (Honeywell style)
         [ RED ]
           ^
          plane

  - The pilot sees "I cannot see behind this storm" and does not plan a
    route through the shadow.
  - Easy to add: the data is already computed.

    TREE
    Each beam
       +-- add up the loss through the rain
       +-- loss small -> normal picture
       +-- loss big   -> mark the far part: yellow arc / cyan area
                         ("radar blind here")

27.3  STORM TOP LABELS AND LIGHTNING ICON                   (+3 points)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STORM TOP LABELS - WHAT IT IS
  Modern radars (Collins MultiScan, Boeing 787) write a small number on
  each big storm: its top height, e.g. "+380" = top 38,000 ft
  (or relative: "+120" = 12,000 ft above you).

WITHOUT IT (now) - what is wrong
  - The pilot sees a red cell but not how TALL it is.
  - Cannot judge: can I climb over it? (Rule: keep at least 5,000 ft
    above a big storm top - often impossible.)
  - To find the top he must tilt up and down by hand and work it out.

WITH IT - what would be right
  - The app now KNOWS each storm's top (highest cloud, from the cloud
    shapes). Just draw it:

         [ RED ] +380        <- top 38,000 ft
           [ YEL ] +180
             ^
            plane at FL250

  - Pilot sees at once: the 38,000 ft storm cannot be overflown; the
    18,000 ft one is below him.
  - Easy to add: the data is already there (cloud tops).

LIGHTNING ICON - WHAT IT IS
  Honeywell IntuVue draws a lightning symbol on cells that are likely to
  have lightning.

WITHOUT IT (now) - what is wrong
  - The app reads only FlightGear's LAST lightning strike and uses it to
    make the nearest cloud a red storm core. Nothing is DRAWN.
  - Lightning is a sign of a strong, active storm (updraft, hail,
    turbulence). The pilot gets no hint.

WITH IT - what would be right
  - Export ALL strikes from FlightGear (script), count recent strikes per
    storm, draw a lightning symbol on active storms.

         [ RED ] (lightning)   <- active storm: more dangerous
           [ RED ]             <- no strikes

    TREE
    Each storm (Cb)
       +-- top from its clouds   -> label "+380"
       +-- strikes in the last minutes?
              +-- yes -> lightning icon
              +-- no  -> no icon

27.4  VERTICAL (SIDE) VIEW / VERTICAL PROFILE                (+4 points)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
  Airbus A350/A380 ("VD"), Boeing 787, Honeywell IntuVue: a second small
  picture showing the weather FROM THE SIDE along your track (or any
  chosen line): ground, storm base, storm TOP, freezing level, and your
  plane's height line.

WITHOUT IT (now) - what is wrong
  The normal radar shows weather from ABOVE only, at the heights the beam
  happens to pass. The pilot must guess the height of everything:

    Radar from above:  [ RED ]  ahead
                        ^
                       plane
    Question: will I go OVER it, UNDER it, or THROUGH it?
    -> unknown. Pilot must tilt up / down many times and do sums.

  Danger: the beam can go OVER a storm (too high tilt) or the storm can
  be BELOW you and grow up into your path.

WITH IT - what would be right

    SIDE VIEW along the track:

    40,000 ft |            ___
              |           |###|  <- storm top 38,000 ft
    FL250 ----|---plane---|###|------------  <- your height line
              |           |###|
    freezing  |- - - - - -|###|- - - - - -  <- freezing level (dashed)
              |      [##] |###|
     ground   |______[##]_|###|____________
              0 NM       20 NM          40 NM

  - Pilot sees at once: the storm at 20 NM goes from the ground to
    38,000 ft -> must go AROUND, cannot climb over.
  - The small cell at 12 NM is below the freezing level and below the
    plane -> no danger at FL250.
  - The app already has what it needs: volume base and top, cloud tops,
    freezing level per area, the beam model.

    TREE
    Side view along the track
       +-- for every NM ahead and every 1,000 ft of height:
       |      what echo would the radar see there?
       +-- draw: ground, echoes (same colours), freezing level line,
       |         your height line
       +-- pilot decides: over / under / around
