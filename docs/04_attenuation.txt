ATTENUATION (HEAVY RAIN HIDING WHAT IS BEHIND IT)
=================================================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
The radar beam loses power when it goes through rain. Every drop takes a
little bit. Through heavy rain, so much is lost that what is BEHIND the
rain looks weaker than it really is - or is not seen at all.

Example: like a torch in fog. The first fog patch is bright, but a second
patch behind it looks dim, because the light was used up by the first one.

Danger: a big storm hidden behind a smaller red cell can look green or
black. Pilots call the black area behind a storm a "RADAR SHADOW". The
Airbus note says: never fly into a shadow behind a storm.


2. HOW A REAL RADAR DOES IT
---------------------------
  - Aircraft radars use X-band (about 9.3 GHz). X-band is strongly
    attenuated by heavy rain. The loss counts twice: going out AND coming
    back.
  - Light rain: almost no loss. Heavy rain (50 dBZ): several dB per NM.
    Through a few NM of a storm core, 20-30 dB can be lost - a red storm
    behind becomes green or nothing.
  - A WET RADOME (water on the nose cone) also weakens everything.
  - Some radars partly fix it:
      Collins: "Path Attenuation Compensation" (PAC) - makes the far echoes
      stronger to make up, and draws a YELLOW ARC at the edge of the screen
      where it could not see far enough.
      Honeywell: "REACT" - shows areas where the radar cannot see any more.
  - Pilots are taught: if there is a black area behind a red cell, assume
    it is NOT clear.


3. HOW OUR APP DOES IT   (changed 25 Nov 2026: real X-band loss + yellow arc)
----------------------
Code: radar.py, scan(), the "if attenuation:" part, and
      rain_loss_db_per_nm(). Yellow arc: display.py, _draw_pac().
Switches (under the knobs):
  "Simulate attenuation"                          on by default
  "Blind-area warning (yellow arc, newer radars)" off by default
      off = OLDER radar: full loss, nothing added back, no warning
      on  = NEWER radar (Collins PAC style): adds back up to 20 dB,
            yellow arc where it still cannot see

  For every radar line, going out from the plane, step by step:

  plane --> step --> step --> step --> ...
              |
              +-- rain here?  echo (dBZ) -> rain rate (mm/h)
              |                (Marshall-Palmer: Z = 200 x R^1.6)
              +-- loss here = real X-band loss, out AND back
              |                (ITU: 0.0101 x R^1.276 dB/km, x2, per NM)
              +-- add it to the total lost so far on this line
              |
              +-- OLDER radar:  everything behind is weaker by the total
              +-- NEWER radar:  total minus 20 dB (added back)
                                total above 20 dB?  -> BLIND on this line
                                                    -> YELLOW ARC on the
                                                       outer ring there

  Loss per NM (two-way):
      dBZ    colour     NOW (real)    BEFORE (old formula)
      30     green/yel    0.14           0
      40     red          0.85           0.5
      45     red          2.1            0.75
      50     magenta      5.3            1.0     <- 5x stronger now
      55     magenta     13              1.25    <- 10x stronger now

  Where storm 2 appears: ALWAYS at its real distance (the radar measures
  distance by time; attenuation only changes the COLOUR, not the place).

Example (tested): two storms one behind the other, each 0.8 rain (red),
storm 1 at 30 NM, storm 2 at 45 NM:

      no attenuation    storm 1 RED,  storm 2 RED
      BEFORE (old app)  storm 1 RED,  storm 2 RED       (too easy)
      OLDER radar now   storm 1 RED,  storm 2 GREEN     (hidden behind!)
      NEWER radar now   storm 1 RED,  storm 2 RED + yellow arc at its back

  Very heavy storm 1 (magenta, 55 dBZ, 4 NM):
      storm 2 straight behind -> BLACK in both, newer radar draws the arc.
      storm 2 seen past the SIDE of storm 1 -> normal colour.
      So: TURN or TILT to look past storm 1, like a real pilot.

  No voice, no text - only the yellow arc (like the real Collins radar).


4. IS IT CORRECT?
-----------------
  Heavy rain weakens what is behind     Yes
  Light rain almost no loss             Yes
  Ground behind storms also weaker      Yes
  How much loss in very heavy rain      Yes now (real X-band formula,
                                        out and back)
  Snow and ice almost no loss           Yes (only rain counts)
  Storm 2 shown at its real distance    Yes
  Older radar: no compensation          Yes (checkbox off)
  Newer radar: compensation + arc       Yes, simple (fixed 20 dB limit)
  Wet radome                            No
  Honeywell REACT cyan area             No (only the Collins-style arc)
