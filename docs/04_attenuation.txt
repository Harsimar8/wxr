ATTENUATION (HEAVY RAIN HIDING WHAT IS BEHIND IT)
=================================================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
The radar beam loses power when it goes through rain. Every drop takes a
little bit. Through heavy rain, so much is lost that what is BEHIND the
rain looks weaker than it really is - or is not seen at all.

Example: like a torch in fog. The first fog patch is bright, but a second
patch behind it looks dim, because the light was used up by the first one.

Danger: a big storm hidden behind a smaller red cell can look green or
black. Pilots call the black area behind a storm a "RADAR SHADOW". The
Airbus note says: never fly into a shadow behind a storm.


2. HOW A REAL RADAR DOES IT
---------------------------
  - Aircraft radars use X-band (about 9.3 GHz). X-band is strongly
    attenuated by heavy rain. The loss counts twice: going out AND coming
    back.
  - Light rain: almost no loss. Heavy rain (50 dBZ): several dB per NM.
    Through a few NM of a storm core, 20-30 dB can be lost - a red storm
    behind becomes green or nothing.
  - A WET RADOME (water on the nose cone) also weakens everything.
  - Some radars partly fix it:
      Collins: "Path Attenuation Compensation" (PAC) - makes the far echoes
      stronger to make up, and draws a YELLOW ARC at the edge of the screen
      where it could not see far enough.
      Honeywell: "REACT" - shows areas where the radar cannot see any more.
  - Pilots are taught: if there is a black area behind a red cell, assume
    it is NOT clear.


3. HOW OUR APP DOES IT
----------------------
Code: radar.py, scan(), the "if attenuation:" part.
Switch: "Simulate attenuation" checkbox (on by default).

  - Along each beam, going outwards, every piece of rain stronger than
    30 dBZ takes power away:
        loss (dB per NM) = (dBZ - 30) x 0.05
  - The loss adds up. Everything behind (rain, snow, ground) gets weaker.

Example:
  A 50 dBZ storm core, 5 NM thick:
    loss = (50 - 30) x 0.05 = 1 dB per NM, x 5 NM = 5 dB.
  A 45 dBZ storm behind it shows as 40 dBZ: red instead of red/yellow edge.
  With a real X-band radar the loss would probably be bigger (several dB
  per NM), so the storm behind could look yellow or green.


4. IS IT CORRECT?
-----------------
  Heavy rain weakens what is behind     Yes
  Light rain (below 30 dBZ) no loss     Yes, about right
  Ground behind storms also weaker      Yes
  How much loss in very heavy rain      Too gentle (probably 2-4 times
                                        less than real X-band)
  Snow and ice almost no loss           Yes (only rain counts)
  Wet radome                            No
  Warning "can't see behind"            No


5. WHAT COULD BE IMPROVED
-------------------------
  A1. Use a real X-band attenuation formula (stronger loss in very heavy
      rain, counted both ways).
  A2. Draw a "can't see further" mark (like Collins PAC yellow arc or
      Honeywell REACT) where the beam has lost too much power.
  A3. Wet radome option: everything a few dB weaker when flying in rain.
  A4. Teaching button: show the same picture with and without attenuation
      side by side, to see what was hidden.


6. WHAT MORE FLIGHTGEAR CAN GIVE
--------------------------------
  - Rain strength along the whole beam (already used, from the volumes).
  - Rain at the aircraft (/environment/rain-norm): to know if the radome
    is wet (A3).
  - Nothing else is needed - attenuation is pure physics from the rain
    the app already knows.
