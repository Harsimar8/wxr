HOW THE RADAR SCANS, AND "AUTO" TILT
====================================
(Read 06_tilt.txt first if "tilt" is new to you.)


PART 1 - THE RADAR MOVES IN ONE DIRECTION ONLY: LEFT <-> RIGHT
-------------------------------------------------------------
Think of a torch fixed on a windscreen wiper.

  Seen from ABOVE:

               left  \   |   /  right
                      \  |  /
                       \ | /
                        \|/
                       PLANE

  - The antenna swings from LEFT to RIGHT and back (90 deg each side).
  - One swing takes about 4 seconds.
  - Behind the swing line, the picture on the screen is drawn again.

  It does NOT swing up and down by itself. Up/down is the TILT, and the
  tilt stays the SAME during the whole swing.

  So: left-right = automatic swing.   up-down = tilt (set by knob or AUTO).

Our app:  exactly this. Swing +/- 90 deg, 45 deg per second (4 s per
          swing), one tilt for the whole swing.            -> like real.


PART 2 - WHY TILT MUST CHANGE
-----------------------------
The right tilt depends on TWO things:

  1. HOW HIGH you fly       (high up -> aim more down)
  2. HOW FAR you look       (range 40 NM or 160 NM?)

Aim too high: the beam passes OVER the storm -> storm missed.
Aim too low:  the beam hits the GROUND -> screen full of ground echo.

The pilot would have to turn the knob every time altitude or range
changes. AUTO does it for the pilot.


PART 3 - WHAT "AUTO" DOES (OUR APP)
-----------------------------------
ONE simple rule:

  "Aim so that the LOWEST part of the beam just touches the ground a
   little BEYOND the edge of the screen."

  Seen from the SIDE:

   PLANE >==========______________
           \\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\_______   <- beam (cone)
                  \\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\_____
                                    \\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\
   ground ______________________________________|_____X____
                                     edge of screen   ^
                                                lowest part of beam
                                                touches ground here
                                                (a bit past the edge)

  Why this point?
   - Up to the edge of the screen the beam is still in the air -> you
     see WEATHER, not ground.
   - The beam is as LOW as possible -> it sees the lower, wet, strong part
     of storms (not over their tops).

  "A bit beyond the edge" = 1.15 x range.
     range 80 NM -> touches the ground at 92 NM.

  Rounding: the knob moves in 0.25 deg steps. The app always rounds
  UPWARDS (e.g. -1.02 -> -1.00), so the beam never dips into the ground.

  On the runway (height 0): the ground is right there, so AUTO points the
  beam's CENTRE a bit UP (+2.75 deg) so its LOWEST part is level.


PART 4 - EXAMPLES (real numbers from the app)
---------------------------------------------
  Height       Range     AUTO tilt    Meaning
  ----------   -------   ---------    --------------------------------
  30,000 ft     80 NM    -1.00 deg    a little down
  35,000 ft    160 NM    -0.25 deg    looking far: almost level
  30,000 ft    320 NM     0.00 deg    level (earth curves away anyway)
  on runway     40 NM    +2.75 deg    centre up, lowest part level

  Change range from 80 to 320 NM at 30,000 ft: tilt goes -1.00 -> 0.00.
  You did nothing; AUTO did it.

  On the screen the tilt shows with an "A" (e.g. -1.0A) when AUTO is on.
  Untick AUTO to use the knob yourself.


PART 5 - WHAT REAL RADARS DO (better than ours)
-----------------------------------------------
  a) Simple AUTO (older radars): same rule as our app.        = ours

  b) Collins "MultiScan": uses TWO tilts. One swing high, next swing low,
     and the computer joins the two pictures. Two looks = fewer storms
     missed.                                                  ours: 1 tilt

  c) Honeywell "RDR-4000 / IntuVue": swings at MANY tilts, one after the
     other, and remembers everything in a 3D box. Then it draws the
     weather at the heights where the plane will fly. The pilot needs no
     tilt at all. It can also show a SIDE VIEW of the storm.  ours: no

  d) Both b) and c) know where mountains are (terrain database) and
     remove the ground echo.                    ours: only draws mountains

  e) Real antennas stay LEVEL when the plane turns or climbs (stabilised,
     using pitch and roll).   ours: the app thinks the plane is always level


PART 6 - IS OUR APP CORRECT?
----------------------------
  Left-right swing, 4 s                     YES
  AUTO from height and range                YES (like simple AUTO)
  Stays level in turns (pitch/roll)         NO  - plane always level
  Aims higher over mountains                NO
  Two tilts / 3D box                        NO  - one tilt


PART 7 - WHAT COULD BE IMPROVED
-------------------------------
  T1. Pitch/roll: read them from FlightGear or the other simulator, keep
      the beam level. Switch to turn it off as a lesson (in a turn, one
      side of the screen fills with ground).
  T2. AUTO over mountains: use the terrain heights we already get, so the
      beam aims above the highest ground.
  T3. Two tilts like MultiScan.
  T4. 3D box + side view like RDR-4000.


PART 8 - WHAT FLIGHTGEAR / THE SIMULATOR CAN GIVE FOR THIS
----------------------------------------------------------
  /orientation/pitch-deg, /orientation/roll-deg  -> T1
  /position/altitude-agl-ft (height above ground) -> AUTO
  Terrain heights (already sent by wxradar_export.nas) -> T2
  Vertical speed, autopilot altitude -> which heights matter (T4)
