WINDSHEAR (PREDICTIVE WINDSHEAR, PWS)
=====================================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A MICROBURST is a strong downdraft under a storm. The air falls to the
ground and spreads out in all directions, like water from a tap hitting a
sink.

A plane flying through it on approach:
  1. first gets a HEADWIND  -> speed goes up, plane goes high,
  2. then a DOWNDRAFT       -> pushed down,
  3. then a TAILWIND        -> speed drops a lot, plane sinks.
Low down, near the runway, this can crash an aircraft. That is why
windshear warnings exist.


2. HOW A REAL RADAR DOES IT
---------------------------
Predictive Windshear (PWS) is a special mode of the weather radar.
  - Works by itself below about 2,300 ft above the ground, whatever mode
    the pilot selected.
  - Scans 5 NM ahead, +/- 40 degrees.
  - Uses DOPPLER on the rain drops: drops on one side of the cell rush
    TOWARDS the plane (headwind), on the other side AWAY (tailwind).
  - From this it computes the "F-factor": how much the wind change will
    rob the plane of energy. About 0.1 - 0.15 averaged over about 1 km
    is dangerous (the exact certified number is not public).
  - Needs SOME rain to measure. A DRY microburst (no rain) is invisible
    to it.
  - Alerts only below 1,200 ft:
      WARNING (red, voice "WINDSHEAR AHEAD" / "GO AROUND, WINDSHEAR
      AHEAD"): close and nearly straight ahead.
         Take-off: within about 3 NM.   Landing: within about 1.5 NM.
         +/- 25 degrees.
      CAUTION (amber, "MONITOR RADAR DISPLAY"): farther out, +/- 40 deg.
  - Alerts are switched OFF:
      on take-off from 100 kt until 50 ft (too busy, can't stop anyway),
      on landing below 50 ft.
  - On the screen: red/black striped icon with two amber lines.
  - ONLY SOME storms have microbursts.

There is also REACTIVE windshear (not the radar): the flight computer
feels the windshear when the plane is already in it.


3. HOW OUR APP DOES IT
----------------------
Code: radar.py, functions windshear() and _f_factor_hazard().

  - Active below 2,300 ft above ground, alerts below 1,200 ft.      [real]
  - Looks 5 NM ahead, +/- 40 degrees.                              [real]
  - Warning: within 3 NM and +/- 25 deg, else caution.             [part]
  - Voice messages, red/black icon, amber lines.                   [real]
  - Needs rain.                                                    [real]

How it finds a microburst - two ways:
  a) REALISTIC mode: the app makes a wind outflow under rain volumes,
     then computes the F-factor along each beam like Doppler, averaged
     over 0.5 NM (about 1 km). F >= 0.13 = hazard.
  b) FLAT / SIMULATED: simple rule. At 500 ft above ground:
     rain 45 dBZ or more AND turbulence 0.6 or more = microburst.

Example: you descend at 1,000 ft, a FlightGear thunderstorm (rain 0.8 =
49 dBZ, turbulence 0.6) is 2 NM ahead.
  49 >= 45 and 0.6 >= 0.6 -> microburst.
  2 NM ahead, straight ahead, below 1,200 ft -> red WARNING
  "GO AROUND, WINDSHEAR AHEAD".
  (Real radar: only if that storm really had a microburst.)

Test in Simulated mode: altitude below 1,200 ft, press
"Add microburst ahead".


4. IS IT CORRECT?
-----------------
  2,300 / 1,200 ft, 5 NM, +/- 40 deg           Yes
  F-factor (Realistic mode)                     Yes, the real method
  Simple rule (Flat / Simulated)                Not Doppler, a stand-in
  Needs rain                                    Yes
  Landing warning only within 1.5 NM            No (always 3 NM)
  Alerts off 100 kt-50 ft and below 50 ft       No
  Only some storms have microbursts             No - EVERY heavy FlightGear
                                                storm counts, too many
                                                alerts
  Dry microburst invisible                      Can't be tested


5. WHAT COULD BE IMPROVED
-------------------------
  W1. Landing: warning within 1.5 NM. Take-off: within 3 NM.
  W2. Switch alerts off from 100 kt to 50 ft on take-off and below 50 ft
      on landing.
  W3. Not every storm is a microburst: only the strongest (heavy rain AND
      strong turbulence AND maybe strong downdraft), or only ones you place
      as "Microburst" on the map.
  W4. Dry microburst test: a downdraft without rain - the radar should
      show NOTHING (good lesson).
  W5. Show the F-factor number in a debug line, to learn what it means.


6. WHAT MORE FLIGHTGEAR CAN GIVE
--------------------------------
  - Volume "lift" value: negative lift = DOWNDRAFT. A heavy-rain volume
    with strong negative lift near the ground is a much better microburst
    sign than turbulence.
  - Wind at each altitude (boundary layer and aloft tables): real wind
    change with height (low-level windshear without storms).
  - Aircraft speed and vertical speed (/velocities/...): needed for
    the F-factor and to know take-off vs landing.
  - /gear/gear/wow (weight on wheels) and airspeed: to switch alerts off
    at the right moments (W2).
  - FlightGear has no real microburst winds. A Nasal script could add a
    wind outflow around a storm, so the FlightGear aircraft really feels
    the headwind/tailwind that the radar predicted.
