HAIL
====

1. WHAT IS HAIL AND WHY CARE
----------------------------
Hail is balls of ice made inside a thunderstorm. A strong updraft (air going
up fast) carries drops high above the freezing level, they freeze, fall, get
carried up again, and grow. Hail can break the windshield and the radome,
and damage engines. Hail can also fall OUT OF THE SIDE or the top of a
storm, into clear air, several miles away from the red area.


2. HOW A REAL RADAR DOES IT
---------------------------
A real aircraft radar CANNOT SEE HAIL ITSELF. It only sees how strong the
echo is.
  - Wet hail (ice with a water skin, low down) gives a VERY strong echo:
    red or magenta.
  - Dry hail (high up, cold) gives a WEAK echo: maybe only green, even
    though it is dangerous.

So pilots use clues (Airbus briefing note "Optimum use of the weather
radar"):
  - Strange shapes: hooks, fingers, U-shapes, scalloped edges = hail likely.
  - Strong echo (red/magenta) HIGH above the freezing level = strong updraft
    = hail likely.
  - Big, fast-changing red areas.

Newer radars (Honeywell IntuVue, Collins MultiScan) draw a HAIL ICON
by themselves. They look at the storm in height and check if strong echo
reaches high above the freezing level. The exact rule is the
manufacturers' secret (not published).

Ground weather radars (NEXRAD, in the USA) use a published rule
(Waldvogel 1979):
  "If the 45 dBZ echo reaches 1.4 km (about 4,600 ft) or more above the
   freezing level, there is a good chance of hail."


3. HOW OUR APP DOES IT
----------------------
Code: radar.py, function hail_cells().

For each FlightGear RAIN volume:
  1. Take its rain strength. Rain 0.8 = about 49 dBZ, rain 1.0 = about 57.
  2. Go up through the volume in 100 ft steps.
  3. Above the freezing level, make the echo 7 dB weaker (ice reflects
     about 5 times less than water).
  4. In Realistic mode, the top of the storm also fades (like real storms).
  5. Find the HIGHEST point that is still 45 dBZ or more.
  6. If that point is 4,600 ft or more above the freezing level -> hail
     icon (white upside-down triangle with dots) at that volume.

Example:
  Freezing level 12,000 ft.
  FlightGear thunderstorm: 0 to 20,000 ft, rain 0.8 (49 dBZ).
  Above 12,000 ft: 49 - 7 = 42 dBZ. That is below 45.
  -> highest 45 dBZ point is 12,000 ft = 0 ft above freezing -> NO HAIL.

  A taller storm, 0 to 40,000 ft, rain 1.0 (57 dBZ):
  Above 12,000 ft: 57 - 7 = 50 dBZ, still above 45, up to about 30,000 ft.
  30,000 - 12,000 = 18,000 ft above freezing -> HAIL ICON.


4. IS IT CORRECT?
-----------------
  Hail icon from the height of the strong echo   Yes, good guess (published
                                                 ground-radar rule)
  Dry hail shows weak                            Yes (the -7 dB ice rule)
  Wet hail shows extra strong                    No (hail is treated like
                                                 rain)
  Works on FlightGear volumes                    Yes
  Works on Simulated storms                      No
  "GR" (hail) in a METAR                         Treated as rain


5. WHAT COULD BE IMPROVED
-------------------------
  H1. Put the hail icon on SIMULATED storms too. The shape scenarios
      (hook, finger, U-shape, scalloped) are exactly the hail storms of the
      Airbus note, so they should show it.
  H2. If the METAR says GR (hail) or GS (small hail), make the storm
      cores a bit stronger (wet hail = very strong echo).
  H3. "Hail outside the cloud": also warn a few NM downwind of a hail
      storm's top (hail falls out of the anvil). Real radars do not show
      this; it would be a teaching extra.


6. WHAT MORE FLIGHTGEAR CAN GIVE
--------------------------------
  - FlightGear has NO hail information inside its volumes. Only rain, snow,
    turbulence, lift, visibility.
  - METAR text: "GR" / "GS" says hail is reported (but not where).
  - Lightning (/local-weather/lightning): where strikes happen = strong
    storm = more hail chance.
  - Temperature by altitude (environment config table): gives the freezing
    level, which the hail rule needs.
  - Volume "lift" (updraft): a strong updraft is what makes hail. A volume
    with big positive lift and heavy rain = better hail guess.
  - We could write a Nasal script that adds our own "hail" value to each
    thunderstorm volume when FlightGear builds it.
