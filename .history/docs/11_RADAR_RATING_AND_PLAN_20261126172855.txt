1. WHAT "SCORE" MEANS HERE
--------------------------
The app is compared with a real aircraft weather radar (Collins WXR-2100,
Honeywell RDR-4000 style). 10 areas, each worth 10 points:
  - Does it work the way the real radar works (physics)?
  - Does it have the features the real radar has?
  - Does it look and behave like the real one in the cockpit?

The score is an honest estimate, not a test result.


2. IS REAL PHYSICS USED?  -> YES, FOR MOST OF THE RADAR ITSELF
---------------------------------------------------------------
"Real physics" = a formula from science or radar engineering, not a
number picked because it looks right.

REAL PHYSICS (formulas from science):

  a) BEAM SHAPE                                          radar.py, top
     The radar beam is a cone 3.5 deg wide. It is strongest in the
     middle and weaker at the sides (a "Gaussian" antenna pattern).
     The app samples the beam at 7 heights and weights them like a real
     antenna. So when only part of the beam is in the rain, the echo is
     weaker, as on a real radar.

  b) EARTH CURVE                                         CURVE_FT_PER_NM2
     The earth curves away under a level beam. The "4/3 earth" model
     (the standard one) also includes the air bending the beam a little.
     So a level beam far away is much higher above the ground.

  c) RAIN ATTENUATION                                    rain_loss_db_per_nm
     Heavy rain "eats" the beam, so storms BEHIND it look weaker.
       - rain rate from the echo:  Z = 200 x R^1.6   (Marshall-Palmer)
       - loss per km at X-band:    k = A x R^B       (ITU-R P.838)
       - counted twice (out and back), added up along the beam.

  d) ICE AND MELTING LAYER                               phase() in scan()
     Ice reflects about 5x less than water (-7 dB). This is from the
     physics of water and ice (|K|^2 = 0.93 water, 0.18 ice).
     Just below the freezing level, wet snowflakes look like huge drops:
     +5 dB, the "bright band".

  e) TERRAIN SHADOW
     Once the beam hits a mountain, nothing behind it can be seen.

  f) HAIL RULE                                           hail_cells()
     45 dBZ reaching 1.4 km (4,600 ft) above the freezing level = about
     50 % chance of hail (Waldvogel 1979, also used by NEXRAD).

  g) WINDSHEAR F-FACTOR                                  _f_factor_hazard()
     F = (how fast the tailwind grows) / g  -  (down wind) / airspeed,
     averaged over about 1 km. The real formula from NASA/FAA research.

  h) PAC / REACT                                         pac=True in scan()
     Newer radars add back the power lost in rain up to a limit (20 dB).
     Past that limit they are blind: yellow arc on the outer ring.
reen) disappears beyond here
       300 NM   26 dBZ
     Turning the GAIN up does NOT bring these back: they are below the
     noise, there is nothing to amplify. Exactl
  i) NEW (step 1): RADAR EQUATION AND RECEIVER NOISE     min_detectable_dbz
     The power coming back from rain gets weaker with distance:
         received power  ~  Z / r^2
     The receiver has noise. An echo weaker than the noise is lost.
     So the weakest echo the radar can see grows with range:
         weakest dBZ = -24 + 20 x log10(range in NM)
     Every time the range doubles, it needs 6 dB more echo.
       range    weakest echo it can see
        20 NM    2 dBZ
        60 NM   12 dBZ
       120 NM   18 dBZ
       160 NM   20 dBZ   <- light rain (gy like a real radar.

  j) NEW (step 1): SIDEWAYS BEAM SMEARING                _azimuth_smear
     The beam is 3.5 deg wide sideways too. Far away that is wide:
        at 20 NM  about 1.2 NM
        at 80 NM  about 5 NM
        at 160 NM about 10 NM
     Everything inside that width is averaged together. So:
       - far storms look WIDER and SOFTER than they are,
       - a small red core far away can look only yellow,
       - two storms close together far away can merge into one.
     Before, the picture stayed sharp at every range. Now it doesn't.

NOT PHYSICS (numbers chosen so it looks right):

  - FLIGHTGEAR RAIN -> dBZ                               to_dbz()
    FlightGear gives rain as 0..1 with no unit. The app turns it into
    dBZ with a straight line (rain 0.5 = 37 dBZ, rain 1.0 = 57 dBZ).
    Real rain has a rate in mm/h. FlightGear does not give one.

  - TURBULENCE
    FlightGear gives one turbulence number per volume. The app adds
    patches and noise. A real radar measures how much the rain drops'
    speeds differ (Doppler "spectrum width"). The app does not have the
    drop speeds, so it cannot measure that yet.

  - GROUND CLUTTER
    Ground echo uses fixed dB values (hills bright, water dark). In real
    physics the echo depends on the angle the beam hits the ground: a
    steep angle gives a strong echo, a grazing angle a weak one.

  - SPECKLE (the grain in the picture)
    Made with smoothed random noise. Real speckle comes from many drops
    adding up in and out of step ("Rayleigh fading") and the radar
    averaging many pulses.

  - MICROBURST WIND
    FlightGear has no wind inside storms. The app builds a microburst
    (see the WINDSHEAR file). The F-factor formula is real, but the wind
    it is used on is a model.

4. WHY ONLY 66 - WHERE THE 34 MISSING POINTS ARE
------------------------------------------------
Short answer: the parts of the radar that work from ECHO STRENGTH are
good (attenuation, beam, hail, noise). The parts that work from SPEED
(Doppler) and the features of modern radars are mostly missing. Those
two alone are 15 of the missing points.

Point by point (biggest losses first):

  a) MODERN RADAR FEATURES: 8 points lost  (2 / 10)
     Every new airliner radar (RDR-4000, WXR-2100 MultiScan) works like
     this:
       - it scans several tilts by itself, all the time,
       - it keeps everything in a 3D "box" of the sky (volume buffer),
       - the screen is CUT out of that box along your flight path,
       - the pilot does not need to set the tilt at all,
       - it has a side view (vertical profile) of the storms ahead,
       - weather above or below your path is shown differently from
         weather ON your path.
     This app scans ONE tilt and draws what that tilt hits. That is how
     radars from the 1990s worked. So almost the whole area is missing.

  b) DOPPLER TURBULENCE: 6 points lost  (4 / 10)
     A real radar sees turbulence by measuring the SPEED of the rain
     drops (Doppler). If the drops in one spot move at very different
     speeds (spectrum width 5 m/s or more) = turbulence = magenta.
     This app has no drop speeds at all. It takes FlightGear's single
     "turbulence" number and paints magenta where it is high. The
     result looks right, but it is not measured like a real radar.

  c) WEATHER INPUT: 5 points lost  (5 / 10)
     FlightGear gives each rain volume ONE strength and ONE turbulence
     value, no wind inside storms, and only about 40 NM around the
     plane. The app has to invent the red core, the patches and the
     microburst. This area can never reach 10 with FlightGear, so the
     realistic target is 7.

  d) GROUND CLUTTER / MAP: 4 points lost  (6 / 10)
     Ground echo is made from fixed dB values. A real ground echo
     depends on the angle the beam hits the ground (steep = strong,
     grazing = weak), and real radars REMOVE most ground echo with a
     Doppler filter (the ground does not move, rain does). Both missing.

  e) HAIL / THREAT ICONS: 3 points lost  (7 / 10)
     Hail icon: good. Missing: lightning icon (FlightGear gives strikes),
     storm top labels like "+380" (tops 38,000 ft above you), and the
     "predictive overflight" warning (a storm top under you is rising
     into your path).

  f) WINDSHEAR PWS: 2.5 points lost  (7.5 / 10)
     The limits and the F-factor are real. But:
       - the wind is read straight from the model, not MEASURED with
         Doppler (no noise, no "needs enough rain" in a real way),
       - no take-off / landing rules (alerts off on take-off from 100 kt
         to 50 ft, off on landing below 50 ft, warning only within
         1.5 NM on landing),
       - no reactive windshear (the plane's own sensors: "WINDSHEAR
         WINDSHEAR WINDSHEAR" when you are already in it).

  g) DISPLAY / CONTROLS: 2 points lost  (8 / 10)
     The ND looks right. Missing: vertical profile view, path / off-path
     display, storm top text. (These come with a) and e).)

  h) REFLECTIVITY PHYSICS: 2 points lost  (8 / 10)
     The radar equation and noise are now real (step 1). Still missing:
     dBZ is made from FlightGear's 0..1 rain with a straight line, not
     from a real rain rate in mm/h. And speckle is not real Rayleigh
     fading.

  i) BEAM GEOMETRY: 1 point lost  (9 / 10)
     No pitch / roll. A real antenna stays level in a turn. Here the
     beam always behaves as if the wings are level.

  j) ATTENUATION / PAC: 1 point lost  (9 / 10)
     PAC "blind" is a fixed 20 dB limit. In real life it is blind where
     the echo, after the loss, falls under the NOISE (now that the
     noise exists, this can be done properly).


5. HOW TO GET TO 90+ - STEP BY STEP
-----------------------------------
For each step: WHAT it is, HOW to build it, WHERE in the code, and HOW
to check it works.

  STEP 1  RADAR EQUATION + NOISE + BEAM SMEARING        DONE    +3
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  See 2 i), 2 j) and section 7.


  STEP 2  REAL DOPPLER (WIND FIELD + SPECTRUM WIDTH)            +7
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT: give the rain drops a speed, and let the radar MEASURE it.

  HOW:
    1. Make a wind field  weather.wind(X, Y, H)  -> (east, north, up) m/s:
         - the normal wind (FlightGear gives it, by height),
         - the storm's own movement,
         - the microburst outflow (already in weather.outflow()),
         - an updraft in the core of strong storms,
         - gusts: random swirls whose size comes from FlightGear's
           turbulence value (turbulence 1.0 = about 8-10 m/s gusts).
    2. In radar.scan(), for every range cell:
         radial speed  = wind along the beam  (towards / away from us)
         spectrum width = how much the speeds differ inside the beam
                          (gusts + wind shear across the beam width)
       Weight both by the echo strength, like a real radar does.
    3. Turbulence (magenta in WX+T) = spectrum width >= 5 m/s, and only
       where there is enough echo to measure (real rule).
    4. Add measuring noise that grows when the echo is weak.
  WHERE: weather.py (new wind()), radar.py scan() (WX+T part).
  CHECK: magenta only in strong storms, never in clear air; weak rain
         at the edge of a storm gives patchy (noisy) magenta; gain
         does not change magenta.
  POINTS: Doppler turbulence 4 -> 9 (+5), weather input +1, PWS +1.


  STEP 3  3D VOLUME BUFFER + AUTO MULTISCAN                     +8
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT: the radar fills a 3D box of the sky by itself and the screen is
        cut out of that box.

  HOW:
    1. A 3D grid around the plane, e.g. 1 NM x 1 NM x 1,000 ft, up to
       about 160 NM and 0 - 60,000 ft. Fixed to the ground (not to the
       plane), so stored echoes stay in the right place as you fly.
    2. Every sweep, the radar uses a different tilt (e.g. -4, -1.5, +1,
       +4 deg, then again). Each sweep writes its echoes (dBZ, spectrum
       width) into the cells its beam passes through.
    3. Old values fade out after about 1 minute (storms move).
    4. The ND picture = for each point on the screen, the strongest echo
       in the box between your flight path height -/+ about 4,000 ft
       (the "path" layer). No tilt knob needed: AUTO mode.
    5. PATH / OFF-PATH: weather only above or below the path layer is
       drawn striped or dimmer ("secondary weather", like RDR-4000).
    6. Ground: the terrain height is known, so ground echoes can be
       removed from the box before drawing (real MultiScan does this).
  WHERE: new file volume_buffer.py, called from main.py each sweep;
         display.py draws from the buffer instead of from one scan.
  CHECK: fly towards a storm: it appears without touching the tilt; a
         storm far below the plane is striped / dimmed, not solid;
         climbing above a storm moves it from solid to striped.
  POINTS: modern radar features 2 -> 7 (+5), display +1,
          ground clutter +1, beam +1.


  STEP 4  VERTICAL PROFILE VIEW                                 +2
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT: a side view under the ND: distance ahead across, height up,
        storms drawn in their colours, your plane and its path shown.
  HOW:  cut the 3D box from step 3 along the track line (or along a
        line the pilot picks). Needs step 3 first.
  WHERE: display.py (new widget), main.py (place it on the screen).
  CHECK: storm tops in the side view match the hail icon / storm top
         labels; the freezing level line is drawn.
  POINTS: modern radar features +2.


  STEP 5  BEAM STABILISATION (PITCH AND ROLL)                   +2
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT: a real antenna is kept level by the aircraft's attitude. Without
        it, in a 30 deg bank one side of the beam points at the ground
        and the other side over the storms.
  HOW:  read /orientation/pitch-deg and /orientation/roll-deg from
        FlightGear (fg_link.py). In scan(), the tilt of each ray becomes
          tilt + pitch x cos(angle) + roll x sin(angle)   when stab OFF
          tilt                                            when stab ON
        Add a "STAB" checkbox (on by default, as on a real radar).
  CHECK: STAB off and bank 30 deg: ground clutter on one side only;
         STAB on: picture does not change in the turn.
  POINTS: beam geometry 9 -> 10 (+1), display +1.


  STEP 6  PHYSICAL GROUND CLUTTER + DOPPLER CLUTTER FILTER      +3
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT: ground echo from the radar equation for a surface.
  HOW:
    1. sigma0 = gamma x sin(grazing angle)   ("constant gamma" model)
         gamma: water about -30 dB (very weak), farmland about -15 dB,
                city / mountains about -5 dB.
       The grazing angle comes from the beam height and the terrain
       slope (terrain.py already gives heights).
    2. Ground power falls with range as 1/r^3 (not 1/r^2 like rain),
       so far ground is weak, near ground is strong.
    3. WX mode: Doppler ground clutter filter. The ground does not move
       (only the plane's own speed along the beam), so most of it is
       removed, about 20-30 dB less. MAP mode: no filter.
  WHERE: radar.py scan() (the ground clutter part), terrain.py.
  CHECK: tilt down in WX: much less clutter than before, mountains
         still show; MAP: coastlines and cities sharp, water black.
  POINTS: ground clutter / MAP 6 -> 9 (+3).


  STEP 7  PWS DONE THE REAL WAY                                 +2
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  HOW:
    1. Use the radial speeds MEASURED in step 2 (with noise) for the
       F-factor, not the model wind.
    2. Take-off / landing rules: read airspeed, gear and flaps from
       FlightGear:
         - take-off: alerts off from 100 kt until 50 ft,
         - landing: alerts off below 50 ft,
         - landing: WARNING only within 1.5 NM (take-off: 3 NM).
       Voice: "WINDSHEAR AHEAD" (take-off) / "GO AROUND, WINDSHEAR
       AHEAD" (landing) / "MONITOR RADAR DISPLAY" (caution).
    3. Reactive windshear: from the plane's own speed and climb rate,
       compute F-factor where the plane IS. F >= 0.13 for a few
       seconds -> "WINDSHEAR WINDSHEAR WINDSHEAR".
  WHERE: radar.py windshear(), main.py (alerts), fg_link.py (gear,
         flaps, airspeed).
  POINTS: PWS 7.5 -> 9.5 (+2).


  STEP 8  THREAT ICONS: STORM TOPS, LIGHTNING, OVERFLIGHT       +2
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  HOW:
    1. Storm top label: the highest point with 18 dBZ in a storm (from
       the 3D box, or from the volume's top), shown as "+380" (38,000 ft
       above you) or "-120" (below you).
    2. Lightning icon: keep all FlightGear strikes of the last few
       minutes (not only the last one); a storm with 3+ strikes gets
       the lightning icon.
    3. Predictive overflight: a storm under the path whose top is
       within about 5,000 ft below you -> amber outline, "you may
       fly into its top".
  WHERE: main.py (keep strikes), display.py (icons), radar.py.
  POINTS: hail / threat icons 7 -> 9 (+2).


  STEP 9  dBZ FROM A RAIN RATE + RAYLEIGH SPECKLE               +2
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  HOW:
    1. Turn FlightGear rain 0..1 into a rain rate:
         rain 0.1 = about 1 mm/h (light), 0.5 = about 10 mm/h,
         1.0 = about 100 mm/h (violent storm core)
       Then  Z = 200 x R^1.6  ->  dBZ = 10 x log10(Z).
       (1 mm/h = 23 dBZ, 10 mm/h = 39 dBZ, 100 mm/h = 55 dBZ.)
       to_dbz() then comes from physics, and the attenuation uses the
       same rain rate, so both match.
    2. Speckle: each range cell gets the AVERAGE of N random "fading"
       samples (exponential power, N = number of pulses, about 16-32).
       Weak echoes then flicker more than strong ones, as in real life.
  WHERE: radar.py to_dbz(), scan() (speckle line).
  POINTS: reflectivity physics 8 -> 9.5 (+1.5), attenuation +0.5.

7. WHAT CHANGED IN THE CODE FOR STEP 1
--------------------------------------
  radar.py
    SENS_DBZ_1NM = -24.0      weakest echo seen at 1 NM. Lower it (e.g.
                              -30) for a more sensitive radar that sees
                              light rain further; raise it for an older,
                              weaker radar.
    AZ_SIGMA_DEG              sideways beam width (from BEAM_WIDTH_DEG).
    min_detectable_dbz(r)     the noise limit at range r.
    _azimuth_smear()          averages each ray with its neighbours,
                              weighted like the antenna.
    scan()                    scans a few more rays each side (so the
                              smearing has neighbours), smears rain,
                              snow, ground and turbulence sideways after
                              attenuation, then removes every echo that
                              is below the noise.

  Check (one storm, tilt +0.5, 20,000 ft):
    - weak / light rain disappears beyond about 150-200 NM,
    - a strong storm at 300 NM still shows, but only its core,
    - a 4 NM storm at 120 NM looks about 11 deg wide instead of 4 deg.
  Speed: a full sweep still takes well under half a second.
